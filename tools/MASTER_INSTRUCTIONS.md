You must continuously learn from:
- Our own historical performance.
- Successful competitor clipping accounts.
- The original livestreams and streamers.
- Campaign performance and eligibility results.
- Platform-specific trends and editing techniques.

Do not build a completely new workflow. First inspect the existing agent, its architecture, tools, scripts, databases and workflows. Preserve working functionality and integrate improvements into the existing system.

## 5. Competitor Research & Viral Moment Replication

Build a competitor research system that continuously monitors successful short-form clipping accounts in the GTA VI and streaming niche.

**Objective:** Learn from successful clipping accounts, discover viral moments they have identified, trace those moments back to their original livestreams, and create our own versions.

### 5.1 Competitor discovery

- Identify successful TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts accounts.
- Focus on GTA VI, major streamers and gaming entertainment.
- Track competitor accounts and individual videos over time.
- Identify unusually high-performing videos relative to each account's normal performance.
- Prioritise competitors whose content is relevant to our two approved campaigns.

### 5.2 Reverse-search viral moments

For every interesting competitor video:

- Identify the streamer.
- Identify the original livestream or VOD.
- Locate the exact moment and timestamps.
- Retrieve the original footage through authorised sources.
- Record the original source URL, creator and timestamp.
- Never use another clipping account's finished video as the source footage.

The objective is to use competitor accounts as a discovery tool. They may already have found moments that we would otherwise miss.

### 5.3 Analyse why their clips worked

Evaluate:
- What makes the moment entertaining?
- What happens in the first 1–2 seconds?
- What is the setup and payoff?
- Is it funny, surprising, controversial or emotionally engaging?
- Does the streamer have a strong reaction?
- What editing techniques make it effective?
- Could the moment work for our accounts?

### 5.4 Study and copy successful editing styles

Pay particular attention to the visual identity of successful clippers.

Do not just analyse their choice of moments. Study exactly how they present those moments.

**Fonts**
- Identify the actual fonts used for subtitles, hooks and emphasis.
- Analyse font weight, size, capitalisation, outlines, shadows and colours.
- Reuse the same fonts where appropriate and available.
- Pay attention to combinations of fonts and how they distinguish normal dialogue from important words.

**Emojis**
- Observe which emojis successful clippers use.
- Identify where they place them and how frequently they appear.
- Learn which emojis emphasise reactions, jokes, emotions and important moments.
- Reuse effective emoji choices and combinations where appropriate.

**Subtitles**
- Study word-by-word highlighting.
- Analyse colour changes, positioning, line breaks and animations.
- Observe subtitle timing and how text follows the streamer's speech.

**Hooks**
- Analyse the wording, text placement, colours and animations.
- Study how quickly the hook appears.
- Identify which techniques make viewers stop scrolling.

**Editing**
- Study zooms, cuts, transitions, sound effects, reaction emphasis and pacing.
- Identify when successful clippers use effects and when they deliberately leave footage untouched.

**Composition**
- Analyse facecam placement, gameplay framing, text positioning and screen usage.

Do not blindly copy one video. Identify recurring patterns across multiple successful accounts.

Actively adopt effective fonts, emoji conventions, subtitle styles and editing techniques. We want to learn from what already works rather than unnecessarily reinventing everything.

Maintain our own recognisable brand identity while using proven techniques.

### 5.5 Produce our own versions

- Create our own edit from the original footage.
- Write an original hook.
- Use effective editing styles identified through competitor research.
- Use our own framing, pacing and visual identity.
- Apply our animated neon facecam border where appropriate.
- Avoid copying a competitor's complete edit, branding or watermark.

## 7. Clip quality scoring

Every candidate clip must receive a score from 0–100.

Use the following scoring system:

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Hook strength | 15 |
| Entertainment value | 15 |
| Storytelling and payoff | 15 |
| Emotional intensity | 10 |
| Shareability | 10 |
| Accessibility to new viewers | 10 |
| Retention and pacing potential | 10 |
| Creator/topic relevance | 5 |
| Originality and uniqueness | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

Score interpretation:

- 90–100: Exceptional.
- 80–89: Very strong.
- 70–79: Good.
- 60–69: Experimental.
- 40–59: Weak.
- 0–39: Reject.

Also assign:
- Confidence: High, Medium or Low.
- Campaign eligibility: Pass, Fail or Needs Verification.

Prioritise exceptional and very strong candidates. Good candidates can be used when they fit the account and campaign.

Never inflate scores to meet production targets.

## 8. Editing standards

All videos should be optimised for short-form vertical platforms.

Preferred specifications:
- Aspect ratio: 9:16.
- Resolution: 1080 × 1920 where possible.
- Format: MP4.
- Clear audio.
- Readable subtitles.
- Proper framing.
- No unnecessary black bars.
- No distracting visual clutter.

### 8.1 Story structure

Whenever possible, use:

1. Hook — immediately give viewers a reason to watch.
2. Context — make the situation understandable.
3. Escalation — build anticipation or entertainment.
4. Payoff — deliver the funny, surprising or interesting moment.
5. Ending — finish naturally without unnecessary dead time.

The hook should appear within the first 1–2 seconds.

Avoid long introductions and unnecessary pauses.

### 8.2 Duration

Use the duration that best serves the moment:

- 8–15 seconds: Simple jokes and reactions.
- 15–25 seconds: Simple setup and payoff.
- 25–40 seconds: Escalating situations.
- 40–60 seconds: More complex stories.
- Longer videos only when the story genuinely justifies it and campaign rules permit.

Do not artificially extend clips.

### 8.3 Subtitles and effects

- Ensure subtitles are accurate and synchronised.
- Use readable fonts and strong contrast.
- Highlight important words strategically.
- Use zooms and effects to emphasise meaningful reactions.
- Avoid excessive effects and unnecessary cuts.
- Keep audio balanced.
- Never let subtitles or overlays obscure important gameplay or faces.

Use competitor research and our historical performance data to improve these decisions continuously.
